# What the historical validation drivers establish

The coverage stress test could report success after **14 of its 15 scenarios failed**. Its original CLI also accepted one scenario with 42 covered intervals in 50 draws when the other 14 were near nominal. We replayed both scripts on identical inputs. The corrected runner returns an incomplete status for the first case and rejects nominal coverage in the second. A separate real fit counted the saved interval endpoints directly. ## Coverage as a repeated-sampling measurement

A hit occurs when the marginal ATE interval from a single independent simulated data set includes the known structural effect. With 50 independent draws and true 95% coverage, all 50 intervals cover in **7.69%** of experiments. At most 44 cover in **3.78%**. The old script called 50/50 evidence that intervals were too wide. Its two-sided exact p-value is 0.181. Coverage depends jointly on centering, spread and interval construction. Comparing signed bias, empirical sampling spread and interval width helps locate a failure. [Numerical check](experiments/legacy-coverage.json) · [SciPy exact binomial test and interval](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.binomtest.html)

| Scripted outcomes across the same 15 planned scenarios | Historical result | Updated result |
|---|---|---|
| One 47/50, 14 failed to fit | Success | Incomplete, code 1 |
| One 42/50, fourteen 48/50 | Success | Undercoverage, code 3. Raw coverage p = 0.00319, adjusted p = 0.04783 |
| All fifteen 50/50 | Excess coverage | No individual rejection after correction. Each exact p = 0.181 |
| One 500/500, fourteen 475/500 | Success | Excess coverage, code 2. Adjusted p = 2.62 × 10⁻¹⁰ |

The suite now tests each scenario’s nominal marginal coverage using the hit count and the number of draws. Holm adjustment covers all 15 planned coverage comparisons at the 5% familywise level. The reported exact 95% Monte Carlo intervals are pointwise. A failed cell reserves its place in the family, appears in the output, and takes priority in the exit code. A pattern shared across many weakly informative cells can still merit follow-up. The configurations reuse simulation seeds and a pooled 750-independent-trial calculation would require an additional independence assumption. The old “expected coverage” strings survive as historical annotations. The scientific target is the per-fit nominal coverage. [Regression tests](../../../test/scripts/test_comprehensive_coverage.py) · [Statsmodels implementation of Holm correction](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/generated/statsmodels.stats.multitest.multipletests.html)

At the first Holm threshold of 0.05/15 with 50 draws, a cell must cover in **42 or fewer** draws to trigger a rejection. Even 50/50 cannot trigger an individual excess-coverage rejection at an unadjusted 5% level. A cell with actual coverage of 90% has only a **12.2%** chance of crossing that first threshold. At 85% true coverage the probability is **48.1%**, and at 80% it is **81.0%**. Later Holm steps can become less stringent once another scenario rejects. A non-rejection at this simulation budget leaves sizable deficits compatible with the experiment. [Exact probabilities](experiments/legacy-decisions.json)

All 15 numerical configurations still match the historical driver. Two purported examples with more regressors than observations actually had 20 covariates for 100 observations and 30 for 50. The largest ratio in the entire suite is 0.6. A claim about performance with more covariates than observations requires a different experiment. The driver’s former CSV field `p_value` held the bias p-value. Current exports label `bias_p_value`, `coverage_p_value` and `coverage_p_value_holm` separately. The existing `status` combines the within-cell bias and coverage verdicts. The suite-level coverage decision uses `suite_coverage_status`. [Scenario definitions and exported fields](../../../scripts/comprehensive_coverage_test.py)

**Full default run.** All **750/750 fits** completed, using one thread per worker and 14 workers. **Thirteen of 15 configurations rejected nominal 95% marginal ATE coverage after Holm correction.** The two remaining configurations covered in 45/50 and 49/50 draws, with five covariates and confounding strength 0.5. At n = 2,000 with 15 covariates and strength 1, **13/50** intervals covered (26%) even though the bias test passed. At n = 3,000 with 50 covariates and strength 0.5, **15/50** covered (30%). At n = 5,000 with five covariates and strength 0.5, **42/50** covered (84%, Holm p = 0.01594). The suite returned code 3 for measured undercoverage. These fits used the default binary-treatment EconML forest nuisances, a linear effect model and independent observational draws. They are direct evidence about that combination. [Complete results for all 15 configurations](experiments/legacy-coverage-stress.csv)

**Classical control on the same 750 datasets.** The default generator adds a constant effect, a linear covariate function and independent Gaussian noise. Ordinary least squares with an intercept, treatment and all covariates therefore has a correctly specified outcome equation. Its usual finite-sample Student intervals covered in **45–49 of 50** draws in every scenario and none failed the 15-cell coverage test. An independent FWL calculation matched the fitted Statsmodels coefficient and both interval endpoints on the first draw in each scenario. Eight of the 13 forest cells flagged for undercoverage had passed their separate bias test. These comparisons support using the classical estimator on this known linear benchmark. Applying them to other nuisance classes or real observational studies requires new evidence. [Classical and forest results by configuration](experiments/legacy-classical.csv) · [Paired replay and independent interval check](experiments/legacy_classical.py)

In the 13/50 forest cell, OLS covered **48/50** on the same datasets. On 35 draws only the OLS interval covered, and on two draws both missed. The coverage difference was +70 percentage points (paired Monte Carlo SE 6.55 points). Across draws the forest ATE estimate had SD **0.685**, while its mean interval width was **0.500**. OLS had SD **0.060** and mean width **0.255**. The forest missed below the truth 22 times and above it 15 times. A small average signed error can conceal large errors in opposite directions across datasets. The DGP redraws the outcome and treatment coefficients each time, so these data alone cannot apportion the excess variability between within-DGP fit noise and errors that differ across coefficient draws. [Paired estimates and endpoints for the 50 draws](experiments/legacy-worst-cell-draws.csv)

**Real-fit check.** In the frozen IID design with n = 200, five covariates, effect 2 and confounding strength 0.5, **49/50** saved EconML marginal ATE intervals included 2. The coverage estimate was 98%, its exact 95% Monte Carlo interval was 89.35%–99.95%, and the two-sided coverage p-value was 0.519. Mean signed effect error was +0.03083, its bootstrap interval was −0.01755 to +0.08039, and the bias p-value was 0.226. Mean per-fit interval width was 0.694. This check speaks to these 50 IID draws and fitted specifications. They contain no dates, origins or lagged information sets. The standalone verification also directly recounts the marginal `ate_interval` endpoints. [Real-fit results](experiments/legacy-coverage.json) · [Checker](../../../scripts/verify_coverage_fix.py) · [EconML marginal ATE inference](https://econml.azurewebsites.net/_autosummary/econml.dml.LinearDML.html)

The corrected standalone verification ran all 100 default fits on 2,000 observations with five covariates, effect 2 and confounding strength 0.5. Exactly **92/100** marginal intervals covered. The raw coverage p-value was 0.165, mean signed error −0.0096 and RMSE 0.0558. The original draw sequence and learner seed also produced identical point estimates and interval endpoints in a four-fit, one-worker versus three-worker check. The parallel implementation generates each dataset once, in the original order, and limits each fitting process to one numerical-library thread. ## Historical parameter sweep

The 81-cell sweep varies sample size, covariate count, effect and confounding strength. Its current CSV explicitly identifies the bootstrap interval for **mean simulation bias**, the nominal per-fit coverage level, number of hits and separate bias and coverage tests. For backwards compatibility its `ci_lower` and `ci_upper` columns also contain the limits for mean bias. Within a configuration the generator draws new observations and regression coefficients. These metrics average over those draws. They give neither an interval for a new fitted effect nor evidence about deployment on a later calendar block. [Sweep driver](../../../scripts/bias_validation_simulation.py) · [DGP](../../../dml_ts/validation/dgp_generator.py) · [Bias and marginal coverage definitions](../../../dml_ts/validation/bias_validation.py)

In the November 2025 archived 81-cell sweep, the stored 9%–30% coverage fractions came from placeholder intervals using the Monte Carlo mean’s standard error. The historical aggregate file cannot recover the coverage of the per-fit ATE intervals. Its effect point errors can still support retrospective bias tests. The previously published row-level reconstruction remains the evidence for those original draws. ## 401(k) Lasso uncertainty and diagnostic labels

The November 2025 log reports a pairs-bootstrap SD of **$1,196.85**, a percentile interval of **$2,933.12–$7,643.03**, and an across-seed SD of **$27.66**. In that source, changing the seed simultaneously changed shuffled partitions and the stochastic propensity solver. The range therefore describes joint seed sensitivity. The bootstrap replaces observations and refits the model on each resample. It varies the empirical distribution, and multiple copies of a sampled person can land on different folds. Neither spread is a measure of future-period transport. The companion uses 11 effect modifiers, including two other asset measures. Its specification differs from the author-code 401(k) benchmark. [Historical log](../../../results/lasso_diagnostic/diagnostic_results_20251115_173129.txt) · [Source used for the historical diagnostic](https://github.com/brandon-behring/double_ml_time_series/blob/b45cf52/src/validation/lasso_diagnostic.py) · [Benchmark specification](../2026-11-19_time_series_dml/07-401k-replication.md)

The fold-only diagnostic now changes the outer treatment-stratified partition while fixing the seed for both nuisance learners and the DML estimator. An explicit test inspected all three learner configurations and every held-out row. On the same 250 randomly selected fixture records, two fivefold assignments gave **$8,588.12 and $9,764.95**. The propensity/learner fits emitted **80 convergence warnings** in total. The two estimates exercise the ordinary entry point. Their $832.15 sample SD has only one degree of freedom and provides little precision for partition variability. This subsample supplies no estimate of the full-sample dollar effect. [Measured fit and seeds](experiments/legacy-lasso.json) · [Partition regression test](../../../test/validation/test_lasso_diagnostic.py)

Repeating those same two partition assignments on all **9,915** fixture rows gave **$6,648.94 and $6,717.25**, with a sample SD across the pair of $48.30 and **112 convergence warnings**. The subsample results illustrate how strongly a restricted draw of people can change the scale of the split sensitivity. The two full-data fits share a sample and fixed solver seed. Their difference supplies neither a sampling standard error nor a comparison to the historical log’s 20 jointly varied seeds. [Full-fixture probe](experiments/legacy-full-lasso.json)

The hyperparameter sweep formerly recommended whichever candidate gave the smallest fitted ATE SE “for stability.” It now reports that literal statistic. The fold-count sweep changes both inner tuning folds and outer cross-fitting folds. Its single dataset provides no validation of the coverage of a selected interval. The bootstrap output names the Shapiro–Wilk p-value and the coefficient of variation of the last 100 cumulative means. The latter measures stability of a running average. Numerical solver convergence requires optimizer diagnostics. The historical log recorded five of 1,000 estimates more than three SDs from their mean. That alone supplies no basis to delete these draws. [Diagnostic API](../../../dml_ts/validation/lasso_diagnostic.py) · [Console and text report](../../../scripts/run_lasso_diagnostic.py)
