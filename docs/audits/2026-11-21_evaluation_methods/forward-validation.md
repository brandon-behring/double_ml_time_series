# What the clock permits us to validate

A retrospective coefficient, an outcome predicted as covariates arrive, an earlier causal estimate used as a forecast, and an independently measured later effect have different information sets. For each rolling study record the decision date, treatment date, last feature release, outcome-window end, date the label was available, and first eligible validation date. Count assignment units and treatment contrasts after those restrictions. [Repo timing audit](../2026-11-19_time_series_dml/13-forward-causal-validation.md) · [Frozen forecast scoring](../2026-11-19_time_series_dml/prospective/scoring.md)

| Claim | Honest evaluation |
|---|---|
| “We predicted outcomes well.” | Freeze the learner at the origin, provide only features available at decision time, and score mature observed outcomes. Specify whether covariates arrive during the future block. Report per-origin RMSE/MAE or probability loss and a forecast that could actually have been made then. |
| “We estimated an historical causal effect.” | Use the fold design suited to dependence and unit structure, with identical scored rows and target weights for comparisons. A two-sided retrospective design may use information unavailable to a live forecaster. Its assumptions and purpose must be stated. |
| “We predicted the later effect.” | Freeze a numeric effect or policy rule before later assignment. Compare with a later design-supported estimate of the same intervention, target population, units and horizon. Account for error in the later benchmark and any covariance with the earlier calculation. |
| “The effect disappeared.” | Report the earlier-minus-later estimate with uncertainty. Inspect later support and the ability to detect a change of scientific interest. Failing to reject zero in the last window cannot establish disappearance. |
| “The procedure gives reliable alerts.” | Specify the change that matters, permitted looks, reset behavior and baseline. Simulate the whole monitoring rule under a stable effect and under meaningful changes. |

## Three concrete checks

**Training labels can cross the split.** With 80 ordered dates, a four-date outcome horizon, and a zero-gap temporal split, the last **four training labels** mature on or after the next block starts. A four-date gap eliminates all four in this regular calendar. Production comparisons also need reporting delay and historical data vintages. FRED’s first-release GDP exercise can validate observable forecasts. The Italian GDP shock exercise needs known historical quarters, nonoverlapping raw windows where warranted, and assumptions about shock identification. [Probe and receipt](reproduction/design-probes.json) · [GDP vintages](../2026-11-19_time_series_dml/forward/gdp.md) · [Italy comparison](../2026-11-19_time_series_dml/adversarial/empirical.md)

**The clock used by a variance estimator can differ from the scientific clock.** I inserted 300 empty calendar days halfway through 80 score observations. The panel date-HAC result remained **0.183998** with four lags in both runs. The implementation orders *observed* dates before aggregating scores. This is a reasonable trading-session clock if that is the target. Physical-time dependence across a 300-day break requires an explicit elapsed-time model. Whole-unit fixed-effects validation can also center a held-out person's trajectory using their held-out observations; that within-person projection is a retrospective operation. [Panel implementation](../../../dml_ts/dml/_panel.py) · [Receipt](reproduction/design-probes.json)

**A negative R² can coexist with a useful live forecast.** For outcomes 8, 10, 12 and forecasts 6, 8, 10, RMSE is 2. The repo's R² is −0.5 relative to the realized block mean of 10. Relative to a frozen zero forecast, squared-error skill is +0.961. Which number is relevant depends on the intended comparator. For a fair coin treatment the right prediction is 0.5, irrespective of whether a model can classify individual assignments. ## Examples where time changes the conclusion

- **Effect break.** In the 1,200-date simulation the frozen tree estimate at the first post-break origin averages +1.049, the later effect is −0.5, and the separate later estimate averages −0.420. In the next block, predictive MSE falls while the frozen effect still has the wrong sign. [All block results](../2026-11-19_time_series_dml/paired/rolling.csv)
- **Treatment support.** In London's Q4, two high-price days and seven low-price days had no common start hour. There is no same-slot Q4 contrast to score against the annual eight-slot effect. In Bangalore's August, 20 high-rate and 18 control commuters permit a contrast, but the adjusted SE was 20.39 points. September has no observed high-rate commuters. These are different reasons for an inconclusive result. [Field evidence](../2026-11-19_time_series_dml/field/README.md)
- **Forecast release.** The Kenyan fertilizer offers preceded both observed adoption outcomes. Treating the second assignment as a prospective test of an effect estimated at the first offer uses unavailable information. Cricket and GSS instead preserve issuance records; upcoming results remain pending. [Fertilizer](../2026-11-19_time_series_dml/forward/fertilizer.md) · [Prospective records](../2026-11-19_time_series_dml/prospective/README.md)

Compare losses on identical validation dates, with the same horizon and baseline. Origins often share training data, outcome windows and assignment units. Report the number of independent paths or clusters behind uncertainty. Three correlated calendar blocks do not warrant a three-observation independent test for a general change.
