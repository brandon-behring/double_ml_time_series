# What to predict, what to score and when to stop

Start by writing one sentence specifying who could have received which intervention, when, for what outcome window, and how eligible units are weighted. Two analyses can have identical features, learners and code while estimating different effects. The residualized slope, offer ATE, local effect among compliers, total effect of yesterday's treatment and value of a learned policy each need their own reference. [Identification by design](identification.md) · [Study inventory](study-inventory.md)

| Step and prediction | Permissible model flexibility | Validation on information available at the origin | What would weaken the causal claim? |
|---|---|---|---|
| PLR outcome nuisance, ℓ(H) = E[Y given H] | A regression for the numeric conditional mean, or a probabilistic mean for a binary outcome. Separate model from the treatment learner. | Per-fold outcome MAE or MSE, improvement over a frozen historical baseline, residual drift by date and treatment stratum. | Outcome residuals and treatment residuals align through a shared omitted feature. Outcome prediction alone cannot diagnose this. |
| PLR treatment nuisance, m(H) = E[D given H] | Numeric regression for a dose. Probability or class-probability-weighted mean for discrete treatment. Known randomization probabilities often remove the need to estimate assignment. | Held-out dose MSE or propensity Brier/log loss and calibration. Report variation in D − m(H), support by origin, arm, event and cluster. | Vanishing residual variation, near-deterministic assignment, poor calibration, or post-treatment controls. Accuracy of class labels cannot substitute for probabilities. |
| PLR effect, sum of residual D times residual Y divided by sum of squared residual D | The final slope is scalar. Nonlinear response in the treatment itself or prespecified effect modification changes the model and its target. | Against known truth report bias, RMSE and coverage. Against a classical calculation compare exactly matched rows, nuisance predictions and weighting. For a frozen future slope compare to a later identified effect. | An intercept-free residual moment can be solved in sample even with hidden confounding. The evaluation moment must use independent scores if used to compare candidates. |
| Interactive binary ATE or AIPW, μ₁(H), μ₀(H), e(H) | Separate arm regressions or a regression with interactions, plus a propensity or known design probability. Predicted counterfactual means are needed on both arms' covariates. | Report losses within factual arms and overlap in counterfactual regions. Check both arm models, weight tails, clipping, eligibility and the targeting weights. | Good prediction in a common arm masks lack of support in the other. The marginal ATE and variance-weighted PLR slope can rank learners differently. |
| IV and local effects, instrument probability, reduced form and treatment first stage | Flexible conditional means can remove dependence on observed pre-instrument variables. Choose the valid orthogonal IV score for the specific target. | Report reduced form and first stage separately on matched rows and horizons, support by Z, reference IV fit and weak-identification sensitivity. | Exclusion or independence fails, instrument is weak, or normalization changes between periods. A later association of Z with Y cannot by itself identify a new complier effect. |
| Recursive g-estimation, E[final Y given Hₜ] and E[Dₛ given Hₜ] at each required treatment time | Allow role-specific regressors for the conditional means. The native panel model fits one linear blip per period and recursively removes later contributions. | Split full trajectories by unit, or respect origins in series mode. Check each horizon's nuisance loss, residual treatment variation, response-vector error and covariance on a known dynamic system. | Adjusting for an intermediate state when targeting the total lagged effect can remove the mechanism of interest. A scalar lag-adjusted slope is insufficient validation of a vector of dynamic effects. |
| CATE or effect ranking, τ(H) | Effect regressors can include linear interactions or a forest. Freeze the trained rule and all tuning before evaluating it. | Known conditional-effect RMSE where available, outcome-based best linear projection (BLP) or subgroup contrasts on held-out assignments, ranking and uncertainty by independent unit. | A score can miss an orthogonal modifier, perform poorly in sparse strata, or rank well while predicting effects on the wrong scale. |
| Policy rule, expected benefit minus cost under a fixed policy relative to a named comparator | Any learned decision rule consistent with the intervention, information set, safety constraints and treatment support. | Held-out randomized or design-supported value, coverage of the decisions the new policy would take, policy regret on known truth, evaluation-unit uncertainty. | A positive average treatment effect need not imply positive incremental net benefit at a given cost. In adaptive experiments the logged policy and its probabilities are part of the design. |

## What the public code actually accepts

Ordinary `double_ml` exposes separate cloneable sklearn outcome and treatment models, as well as named presets. Probability-producing estimators can be used for numeric class labels. The named scalar temporal interface exposes separate choices among ridge, lasso, random forest and gradient boosting. A result from the audit's custom spline/boosting comparison does not automatically give that estimator every sklearn constructor. The public panel and dynamic estimators have their own model signatures and transformations. Inspect the specific entry point before promising interchangeability. [Ordinary PLR](../../../dml_ts/dml/double_ml.py) · [Scalar and panel interfaces](../../../dml_ts/dml/temporal_plr_dml.py) · [Recursive dynamic estimator](../../../dml_ts/dml/dynamic_g_estimation.py)

A fair comparison holds scored units, dates, pretreatment history, training budget and weights constant. Fit scaling, feature selection, hyperparameters and any stacking weights using only the training portion of each outer fold. Compare arm means, ANCOVA, FE, local projection or a replication of the original estimator on those same rows as appropriate. Reserve a final untouched path or seed cohort if a metric or selection rule was chosen after reviewing performance. ## What result would change the method choice?

- **Repeated observational draws with known assignment and outcome mechanisms.** In ACIC design 30, both prediction models beat their fold-training mean baselines, yet all 100 intervals missed a constant effect of 10. Holding the arm models fixed and changing only the treatment probabilities cut effect RMSE from 11.84 to 2.22 on 50 evaluation draws. [Coverage study and scope of the diagnostic](../2026-11-22_acic_coverage/README.md)
- **One randomized election or water mailing.** If interacted ANCOVA matches the flexible method's precision on identical assignments, the extra model needs an independent gain on a declared outcome or policy target to justify replacing the simpler benchmark. In turnout the clustered SEs were 0.32252 and 0.32302 for interacted ANCOVA and cross-fitted ridge respectively. [Matched empirical comparison](../2026-11-19_time_series_dml/adversarial/empirical.md)
- **Stable nonlinear confounding with repeated dates.** Require lower paired effect loss on new paths, credible interval calibration and adequate support. At 1,200 dates changing both nuisances improved accuracy substantially, but 80% coverage for nominal 95% intervals leaves inference unresolved. [Measured factorial](nuisance-prediction.md) · [Covariance evaluation](uncertainty.md)
- **A proposed future deployment.** Write the frozen effect or policy to a dated record before the validation arm matures. Compare with zero, previous effect and a simple design-based predictor on eligible later origins. Report how many changes could have been detected, differences in target mix, and the uncertainty of the benchmark. Determine the minimum meaningful difference from the application's units and costs.
