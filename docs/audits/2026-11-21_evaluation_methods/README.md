# Adversarial audit of DML evaluation across the repository

**Use forward validation to answer a dated question.** It can measure how well a nuisance learner predicts mature outcomes or treatments with the information available then. It can also evaluate an effect forecast if an earlier estimate was frozen and the later study independently identifies a comparable effect. A later observation reveals only the outcome under the treatment actually received. Good prediction, a stable coefficient and an accurately reproduced paper each establish something different. [Evaluation by model component](evaluation-matrix.md) · [Forward designs and examples](forward-validation.md)

The audit completed November 22, 2026, after a fresh 600-partition 401(k) refit, 40 registered study commands and 38 standalone replays. [Final reproduction evidence](historical-reproduction.md)

## Findings that change how to read the results

| Decision | What the audit found |
|---|---|
| Use flexible adjustment when the comparison earns it. | With nonlinear confounding, 1,200 dates and matched forward folds, effect RMSE was **0.600** with linear nuisances, **0.434** with only the outcome learner changed to boosted trees, **0.196** with only the treatment learner changed, and **0.079** with both changed. Nominal 95% HAC coverage was only **80.0%** even in that last case. In the randomized turnout comparison, interacted ANCOVA and cross-fitted ridge had nearly identical household-clustered SEs, 0.3225 and 0.3230 percentage points. [Matched simulations](nuisance-prediction.md) · [Matched field estimates](../2026-11-19_time_series_dml/adversarial/empirical.md) |
| Score the effect the decision actually uses. | In the break simulation, selecting a model pair by the preceding block's outcome prediction error gave **0.532** RMSE for the next block's fitted effect on fresh paths, against **0.338** for a prespecified tree pair. These effects were estimated after the next outcomes matured. The separate frozen effect forecasts stayed wrong across the break. With perfect evaluation nuisances, two candidate predictions also rank in opposite order under R-loss and population ATE error. [Fresh-seed selection and targets](effect-metrics.md) |
| Require uncertainty to earn its nominal label. | On a positive control with serial scores, Chapter 5 coverage improved from **60.5% to 89.0%** with HAC. On the chapter generator it moved from 89.5% to 88.5%. In a separate cross-sectional forest/LinearDML audit, **13 of 15** scenarios rejected nominal coverage after multiplicity adjustment, on a correctly specified linear generator (50 draws per cell, 750 fits, no failures). In one cell the forest intervals covered **13/50** while correctly specified OLS covered **48/50 on the same draws**. OLS had zero rejected cells. [Dependence and repeated looks](uncertainty.md) · [Legacy interval experiments](legacy-validation.md) |
| Replace invalid heterogeneity evidence. | The old Chapter 9 example rejected homogeneity in **2,000/2,000** datasets with a constant effect because it regressed a prediction on itself. The repaired outcome-based, held-out test rejected in **127/2,000** under the null, **1,785/2,000** with an aligned modifier and **125/2,000** with an unaligned modifier. Both editions, the executable listings, and the policy-value calculation were corrected. [Heterogeneity and policy](heterogeneity-and-policy.md) |
| Demand an appropriate simple benchmark. | A script called the largest baseline bias the “best.” Rescoring identical stored estimates changed the DML bias reduction from **+82.15%** against naive OLS to **−359.10%** against controlled OLS. A fair randomized-propensity control also exposed the difference between predicting a hard class and estimating its conditional mean. In Chapter 2’s own seeded example, adjusted OLS gave 2.501 against truth 2.5, while the forest gave 2.407 and its interval missed. [Chapter 2 replay](chapter2-evaluation.md) · [Score derivations and exercise corrections](additional/chapter2-theory.md) [Corrections](corrections.md) |
| Distinguish missing support, low power and evidence of change. | London Q4 had no overlapping high/low event start times. Bangalore August retained both comparison arms but had an SE of 20.39 points. In the reminder calibration, 19% of stable paths raised at least one nominal 5% flag over four evaluable quarters. These require three different interpretations before changing an intervention. [Forward cases](forward-validation.md) · [Uncertainty](uncertainty.md) |
| Use external truth to validate computations. | All 16 ACIC development files reproduced the ordinary PLR score to numerical tolerance. On the high-dimensional constant −3 effect, PLR gave −3.016 and AIPW −3.002. Another file's AIPW estimate was 0.925 against a population ATE of 0.191 (SE 0.704). These files include counterfactual means but have no natural time axis. [External known-truth control](external-control.md) |

A separate data-splitting check followed the original household IDs through the 401(k) case bootstrap. **55.08%** of scored copies had another copy of that household in nuisance training. In a constructed ID-memorization control with true slope 2, this gave a better outcome MSE (0.449) but a worse slope (0.898). Grouping copies by original household gave MSE 1 and slope 2. The active Lasso and forest bootstrap diagnostics now keep copies of each original sampling unit together. On three actual Lasso resamples the changes were −$4.19, +$31.80 and −$50.18. These paired results establish sensitivity to fold assignment; they do not measure population coverage. [Bootstrap indices, control and limitations](standalone-reproduction.md)

## Evidence standards for the next application

1. **Before fitting**, freeze intervention, time zero, target population, horizon, assignment unit, eligibility and outcome-availability rule. Establish which design identifies the effect and choose a simple same-target comparator. Record unsupported origins and prior information that would alter the interpretation.
2. **During fitting**, measure both nuisance learners on held-out eligible units, compare effective treatment variation and support by date, and fit preprocessing and tuning inside the training information set. Couple comparisons on identical rows, paths and budgets.
3. **At evaluation**, publish the causal target in outcome units, benchmark uncertainty, interval width, failed replicates and paired uncertainty in comparative performance. Label an empirical result as a design-supported contrast, a replication or a descriptive association as appropriate. For deployment, prespecify a meaningful change and calibrate false alarms and power over the full schedule.

The available evidence supports using temporal DML for specified identified problems with enough informative assignments and a reason for nonlinear adjustment. It also supplies reasons to prefer a randomized arm contrast, ANCOVA, a fixed-effects regression, an IV analysis or a local projection in particular studies. A count of studies with documented prospective validation would require review rules for timing and eligible studies. The 158-paper empirical roster does not establish how many published causal conclusions are wrong. [Study decisions](study-inventory.md) · [Journal roster and limitations](additional-materials.md)
