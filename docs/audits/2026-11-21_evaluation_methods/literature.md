# Research that changes an evaluation decision

The most consequential addition to the review is work on **choosing a causal learner**. A predictor can perform well on factual outcomes while ranking treatment-effect models incorrectly. A causal proxy loss can itself target a different population weighting from the effect of interest. The repo now has numerical examples of both problems. [Learner experiments](nuisance-prediction.md) · [Effect losses](effect-metrics.md)

This was a targeted primary-source search through November 21, 2026. We searched by the decision being made, including DML hyperparameter tuning, R-loss and CATE model selection, policy ranking, forecast comparison, dependent scores, missing outcomes, weak instruments and simulation evaluation. We compared live proceedings, journal, author and experiment pages with the repo's existing bibliography and earlier source review. Several methods, including stacking and impulse-response DML, already appear in the previous review. The contribution here is to say what a source licenses us to test. The search has no systematic database denominator and supplies no estimate of how many economics papers made a causal error. [Earlier review](../2026-11-19_time_series_dml/05-literature.md) · [Earlier source verification](../2026-11-19_time_series_dml/literature-source-checks.md)

Compare tuning procedures on paired effect errors where truth is available. A relationship observed across their generators cannot justify selecting the most favorable causal estimate on the final test sample. [Paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v236/bach24a.html) |

Compare rankings against known CATE, population ATE, and policy regret separately. Repository experiments show a different ranking even with perfect evaluation nuisances. [R-learner](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/asaa076) · [Model selection study](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/curth23b.html) · [Causal risk evaluation](https://doi.org/10.1093/gigascience/giaf016) |
| Vansteelandt & Dukes derive interpretable targets under working-model misspecification. | Calculate which population the scalar PLR coefficient weights. In the reminder experiment the conditional effects can stay fixed while changing randomization probabilities changes the native slope. | Chernozhukov, Demirer, Duflo & Fernández-Val develop inference on features of heterogeneity in randomized experiments. Yadlowsky and colleagues develop inference for treatment-prioritization rules. | Freeze a score on honest training data and evaluate it against an outcome-based signal. A positive projection slope, a useful ranking and positive net policy value are three distinct claims. Chapter 9 now exercises all three distinctions. | Giacomini & White compare predictive performance conditional on available information. Ballinari & Wehrli analyze orthogonal estimation of impulse responses to a sequence of discrete treatments under time-series assumptions. | State the forecast origin, horizon and information set. A frozen effect and an estimate formed after later outcomes mature play different roles. The impulse-response result requires a specified identification and dependence model before it can justify interpreting a local projection. [Predictive ability](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0262.2006.00718.x) · [Impulse-response DML](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10009) |

A simulated linear FE identity validates one operation. [Panel paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/ectj/utaf011) |

## Public data that discriminate between explanations

1. **Known potential-outcome means.** ACIC 2019 exposes the population ATE and the expected outcomes under both treatments for its development files. It permits an independent test of scoring code and factual versus counterfactual error. It has no deployment clock. A row-order split would introduce an artificial temporal question. The challenge's repeated draws within design would be the appropriate extension for sampling coverage. [Organizers' datasets and target](https://sites.google.com/view/acic2019datachallenge/data-challenge) · [Development-file replay](external-control.md)
2. **Repeated randomized decisions with a real clock.** HeartSteps and myBPmyLife have known assignment probabilities, repeated people and proximal outcomes. They let us separate nuisance prediction, an historical offer effect and a forecast scored against a later randomized contrast. Predefine the outcome scale, carrying unit, availability condition, new versus returning participants and the minimum detectable change. The reminder simulation supplies controlled changes that the empirical record alone cannot identify. [HeartSteps replay](../2026-11-19_time_series_dml/pilots/heartsteps.md) · [Reminder calibration](../2026-11-19_time_series_dml/rolling/calibration.md)
3. **Same intervention, less later support.** Use Bangalore for commuter attrition and shared shocks, and London for calendar overlap and quantity shifted across hours. Compare with the published estimator or the same-slot contrast before attempting flexible nuisance estimation. Report both the annual target and which later windows can actually identify it. Both datasets already have runnable receipts. [Field comparison](../2026-11-19_time_series_dml/field/README.md)
4. **A distinct lottery and follow-up design.** The Oregon Health Insurance Experiment's public-use files cover lottery selection, administrative data and survey outcomes. A useful next test is to reproduce the intent-to-treat offer effect with lottery design controls and household clustering, then separately evaluate the coverage effect using the offer instrument. Verify linkage, release dates and comparable outcomes before designating any survey wave a forward validation sample. This is a candidate study. The current audit supplies no fitted Oregon result. [NBER study data](https://www.nber.org/research/data/oregon-health-insurance-experiment-data) · [Repository source ledger](../../research/dataset_ledger.yml)

A useful empirical design has a decisive failure condition. For known truth it can be bias or undercoverage. For a repeated randomized study it can be a materially inaccurate frozen forecast with support and adequate precision. For an observational panel a negative control or plausible selection pattern can undermine a causal interpretation even when every predictive and reproduction check passes.
