A planted randomized experiment on those records separates predictive and causal performance. Each original household received a fresh Bernoulli treatment with probability one half and outcome $Y=2T$. The outcome learner can memorize training households and predicts the population mean of one for a new household. With bootstrap-position folds its prediction MSE improved to **0.449**, yet its partialling-out slope with the true treatment probability fell to **0.898** against the planted effect of **2**. With household folds, MSE was **1.000** and the effect was **2.000** in all 1,000 draws. Seeing a copy allows the outcome model to predict its realized outcome, removing treatment signal from that row’s outcome residual. The historical Lasso uses asset and demographic features without a household ID. This control demonstrates a possible failure with a memorizing learner. The impact on Lasso coverage remains unmeasured. The inferential distinction matters when choosing a bootstrap for another study. Lin and Han’s 2026 preprint, §2.2, weights the original cross-fitted score observations within their original folds and keeps nuisance fits fixed.
