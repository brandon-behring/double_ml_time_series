# Which prediction model improved the effect estimate?

Changing the treatment learner alone reduced effect RMSE from **0.600 to 0.196** in the 1,200-date stationary serial experiment. Changing the outcome learner alone reduced it to **0.434**. Changing both reduced it to **0.079**. All four comparisons started with linear adjustment and used the same observed confounders, data, forward scoring dates and training budgets. These results identify a contribution from each learner in this design and show that the contribution depends on its partner. ## Separate the predictions before scoring their combination

The previous comparison assigned the same model family to both positions. We reconstructed all 48,000 diagonal estimates from the prior archive. The maximum absolute point-estimate difference was below 4 × 10⁻¹¹, compatible with CSV rounding. Within each fold we trained an outcome predictor and a treatment predictor from each of three families. The same six predictions supplied all nine pairings. We crossed 500 paths, 300 and 1,200 dates, four dependence/identification regimes and four split designs, producing **144,000 effect estimates**. Every nine-way comparison is paired on the underlying observations. A second 20-worker run reproduced the two compressed draw files and all three original summary tables byte for byte. ## An exact case where predictive diagnostics cannot distinguish two fits

Take equally frequent independent values of −1 and +1 for a control X, treatment D and noise U, with outcome Y = D + U. The structural effect is one. Give both fits the same treatment prediction X. Give one the outcome prediction X and the other −X. In the public PLR estimator both give outcome MSE **3**, treatment MSE **2**, outcome R² **−0.5**, treatment R² **−1** and SE **0.05608**. Their estimated effects are **1.000** and **0.000**. The squared errors have the same magnitude. Switching the sign changes their correlation across the two prediction problems. [Measured public-API outputs](experiments/counterexamples.json) · [Exact construction](experiments/counterexamples.py)

The mechanism is observable in a simulation. Write the true treatment residual as v = D − m, outcome disturbance as u = Y − ℓ − θv, treatment prediction error as a = m̂ − m, and outcome prediction error as b = ℓ̂ − ℓ. At the true slope the fitted unnormalized PLR score equals vu + v(−b + θa) − au + a(b − θa). Standalone losses discard the signed product in the final term. We checked this identity on every simulated estimate using the known conditional means given the supplied covariates at each date. In the stationary serial tree/tree cell the mean oracle, linear-error and product terms were −0.0041, −0.0029 and +0.0670. In the hidden-common-cause cell, the oracle term itself rose to **+0.993**. Even an analyst given both correct prediction functions would face confounding there. These terms are score units, before division by the treatment residual variance. ## A public classifier returned the wrong quantity

The public ordinary PLR accepted a sklearn classifier as the treatment learner for E[D|X]. Its cross-fit loop used the most likely class. In a balanced 960-observation example with true effect one and treatment probabilities 0.25 and 0.75, this produced **0.7500** and Brier score **0.2500**. The corrected path returns the probability-weighted numeric treatment level. For the same logistic classifier and the same four folds this gives **0.9954** and Brier score **0.1880**. The fitted probabilities differ slightly from their population values. The new result matches an independently wrapped probability predictor. Reference DoubleML also distinguishes probability predictions when using a classification treatment learner in binary PLR. [Before](experiments/classifier-before.json) · [After](experiments/counterexamples.json) · [DoubleML treatment learner contract](https://docs.doubleml.org/stable/api/generated/doubleml.plm.DoubleMLPLR.html)

The native residualization helper now computes the probability-weighted mean on either side of the regression when the estimator is a sklearn classifier. The same change covers the native Robinson estimator and the native dynamic estimator with multiple treatment targets. A registered classifier lacking probability output raises a descriptive error. Regressors continue to return numeric mean predictions through their existing prediction method. The public scalar temporal estimator exposes four regressor names. Its contemporaneous prefitted covariates and lagged treatment inputs still require application-specific checks for pre-assignment availability. For binary assignment report probability calibration, Brier score and the distribution of propensities across eligible units and dates. Known assignment probabilities supply an external benchmark. For continuous assignment report held-out prediction error and residual treatment variation. The temporal estimator’s pooled held-out R² summarizes variation around the pooled evaluation mean. An as-of forecast comparison needs its own earlier-data baseline. The audits of both later causal estimation and effect forecasting use separate endpoints.
