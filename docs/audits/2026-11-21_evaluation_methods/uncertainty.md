# Which uncertainty is being reported?

In Chapter 5, changing only the covariance estimate raised measured 95% coverage from **60.5% to 89.0%** when the treatment and outcome disturbances were both persistent. On the chapter's own generator it changed coverage from **89.5% to 88.5%**. These are paired comparisons on the same draws and the same predictions. The underlying score process predicts why they differ. [Replication-level results](../2026-11-19_time_series_dml/chapter5-draws.csv) · [Generator and paired Monte Carlo errors](../2026-11-19_time_series_dml/04-reproducibility.md)

| Quantity | How to assess it | Failure the audit checks |
|---|---|---|
| Sampling SE of one historical coefficient | Refit on independent simulated datasets or use the assignment distribution. Compare empirical SD of estimates with mean reported SE. Inspect bias and interval width as well as coverage. | A small SE around a biased coefficient can give precise, systematically misleading intervals. |
| Monte Carlo SE of a method comparison | Within each independent simulated path compute the difference in losses or coverage indicators, then divide their sample SD by the square root of the path count. For one success rate use its binomial uncertainty. | Each seed may yield many origins, learner choices and splits. Treating those as independent makes a result look more reproducible than the experiment permits. |
| Random partition or learner variability | Repeat the fit on the same people under recorded seeds. Report partition spread and how within-fit variance enters the repeated-split aggregation rule. | Splits reuse outcomes. Dividing the partition SD by the square root of 100 estimates simulation precision for the split average, a different quantity from sampling uncertainty for the effect. |
| Forecast minus later effect | Estimate both terms jointly if people, assignments or dates recur. Report forecast error in effect units, an interval for the difference and the SE of the later benchmark. | Comparing two p-values or treating a noisy later estimate as exact confuses changes in power with changes in effect. |
| Monitoring across dates | Calibrate the probability of at least one false flag and detection delay at a meaningful change under realistic schedules. Record the number and timing of evaluations. | A pointwise 95% interval can trigger many more than 5% false investigations over a deployment. |
| Conditional effects or policy value | Freeze the score and decision rule. Use independent evaluation units and a design-appropriate influence score or randomization distribution. State whether the target conditions on the trained policy. | Averaging individual lower and upper endpoints or treating the training-selected policy as a prespecified policy on the same data gives misleading uncertainty. |

## Serial dependence belongs to the fitted influence scores

For scalar PLR form the held-out residuals $V=D-\widehat m(X)$ and $U=Y-\widehat\ell(X)-\widehat\theta V$. The influence contribution is $VU/\overline{V^2}$. Its long-run variance adds twice the sum of its positive-lag autocovariances to the lag-zero variance. Outcome autocorrelation is insufficient to determine this sum. HAC bandwidth changes only the estimated covariance when the observations, target and predictions are fixed. Report effective score count, score clock, weighting, bandwidth, interval width, and the dependence assumptions. [Newey and West](https://users.ssc.wisc.edu/~kdwest/publications/1980/A%20Simple%20PSD%20HAC%20Covariance%20Matrix.pdf)

A clean positive control uses independent stationary AR(1) residual-treatment and outcome-error streams, each with autoregressive coefficient 0.6. Their product has correlation $0.36^k$ at lag $k$, so its asymptotic variance multiplier is 2.125. At 1,200 dates the measured oracle multiplier was 2.120 (MCSE 0.136). With serial correlation only in the outcome it was 1.004 (MCSE 0.063), against a theoretical value of 1. Finite-bandwidth HAC averaged 1.969 in the first case. Chapter 5's separate stronger-persistence design likewise shows meaningful undercoverage after HAC. Short samples, nuisance bias and insufficient bandwidth have different remedies. ## Units, clocks and weak denominators

- **Retail and other panels.** Cluster by the independent assignment or sampling units. Common date shocks can induce additional cross-unit covariance. For many units and few dates, an asymptotic two-way normal interval warrants separate small-sample evidence. Unit-grouped folds, forward-date folds and two-way covariance solve distinct problems. In a reproducible date-HAC probe, adding a 300-day hiatus to the same eighty score dates left the SE at 0.183998 with bandwidth four. This implementation weights distances in observed dates. The intended calendar scale must be specified in an irregular panel. [Panel implementation and experiments](../2026-11-19_time_series_dml/panel-inference.md) · [Calendar probe](reproduction/design-probes.json)
- **Few assigned clusters or events.** The day-care analysis can enumerate all 210 allocations of six out of ten centers under the experimental design. London's annual uncertainty draws on 48 selected dates rather than the thousands of meter readings. Its displayed interval uses a finite-date reference under its chosen specification. The London clock match and conditional observation process remain consequential even if the sensitivity intervals agree. [Day care](../2026-11-19_time_series_dml/questions/daycare.md) · [London](../2026-11-19_time_series_dml/field/london/README.md)
- **Weak support or instruments.** Record residual treatment variance, within-stratum arm counts, propensity tails, first stage and reduced form. A ratio can be unstable when its denominator is small even if each nuisance prediction appears plausible. Standard Wald uncertainty around a weakly identified coefficient can be misleading. Weak-IV robust testing answers a different question from changing the second-stage learner. [Fiscal comparisons](../2026-11-19_time_series_dml/classical/README.md) · [Montiel Olea and Pflueger](https://cpflueger.github.io/carolinpflueger_repository/MOP_FINAL_May14.pdf)
- **Different exposures on one path.** Serial response, treatment carryover and overlapping outcomes can induce dependent scores even with independent date-level randomization. Use the recorded assignment law and prespecified exposure histories where possible. For adaptive experiments retain logged assignment probabilities and the evaluation policy's support. [Time-series randomization inference](https://shephard.scholars.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/omnuum7741/files/shephard/files/cause20170718.pdf) · [Adaptive policy evaluation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8054003/)

## How fragile can a forward conclusion look under stability?

For the reminder calendar, the empirical bootstrap refits both parts with common participant weights. In 500 stable panels its four pointwise flag frequencies were 4.8%, 6.0%, 4.0% and 6.4%. **95/500** paths had at least one flag, or 19.0% (MCSE 1.76 percentage points). For a planted decline of 0.10 the January comparison flagged 404/500 paths. This reports both false alarms and detection at an explicit change size. Multiple tests, a different enrolled population, or weaker support can each create apparent instability. An empirical null result needs a scientifically meaningful equivalence margin and adequate precision to support stability. [Paired bootstrap calibration](../2026-11-19_time_series_dml/rolling/calibration.md)

For an individual application publish the later contrast and its precision at every eligible origin, including unsupported origins and failed fits. When estimating coverage, retain the planned denominator and say whether a missing interval counts as a failure to cover. For method selection, show interval width and support alongside coverage error. A method can approach 100% coverage by becoming too imprecise to resolve the scientific question.
