# Which public datasets can test which time-series DML claims?

For the next manuscript revision, use a short sequence of examples. Start with a known-answer experiment on real bike-share histories. Then show how household randomization supplies a reference for electricity demand, how repeated randomization identifies a near-term treatment effect, and where a single policy change exhausts temporal treatment support. Each design tests a different assumption. The May ledger lists 105 entries, including policy studies, macro databases and cross-sectional benchmarks. The repository separately uses Dominick’s orange-juice data in Chapter 4. The four pilots below are additional applications checked against the ledger and chapter sources. Raw observations stay with their publishers. [Existing dataset ledger](../../research/dataset_ledger.yml)

The November 20 follow-up adds ten checksum-pinned replays to the dataset ledger, bringing it to 115 entries. The Italian bank-capital time series, household water trial and voter-turnout trial now have executable benchmarks. [Ten external replication benchmarks](11-external-replication-benchmarks.md)

## Four downloaded and analyzed examples

| Priority for teaching | Units, treatment and outcome | What readers can learn |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Capital Bikeshare | 731 consecutive daily observations. Use the calendar and rental histories to inject a known treatment effect under a specified assignment probability. | Bias and interval coverage can be measured against truth. Contrast a learner's estimate with adjustment using the known probabilities. Observed weather and rental counts also support a separate descriptive example. UCI provides a small CC BY 4.0 ZIP. [Pilot](pilots/bike.md) · [UCI](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/275/bike+sharing+dataset) |
| 2. Norway iFlex | Household-hour electricity consumption, pre-announced incentive events, and contemporaneous controls. First event in Trondheim gives a household-level assignment contrast. | Compare randomized arm means with pre-announcement load adjustment, then change the unit to dates and examine how the question changes. Report the population of opt-in households, event selection, missingness and shared weather. Public version 2 includes a 40.3 MB compressed R workspace and the meter, participant and schedule files. [Pilot](pilots/iflex.md) · [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/8248802) |
| 3. HeartSteps V1 | Within-person randomization of activity suggestions when available. Outcome is log steps during the next 30 minutes. | A published design-based reference makes sample construction and clustered uncertainty independently testable. Assess changes when early decisions are lost to forward fitting, allowing effects to vary with day in study. Public CC BY 4.0 CSV plus original analysis code. [Pilot](pilots/heartsteps.md) · [Data and attribution](https://github.com/klasnja/HeartStepsV1) |
| 4. California Proposition 99 | Annual per-capita cigarette packs in 39 states over 1970–2000. California voters passed Proposition 99 in November 1988. The tax took effect in January 1989. | A pooled number can conceal the absence of treated observations in a training fold. Reproduce synthetic DiD, examine state placebos, and distinguish an average post-policy effect from a single-year gap. The authors supply a public four-column panel. [Pilot](pilots/california.md) · [Authors' data](https://synth-inference.github.io/synthdid/reference/california_prop99.html) |

The source lists half-hourly kWh, a roughly 765 MB ZIP, a separate day-ahead tariff workbook and a Creative Commons Attribution licence. [UK Power Networks data](https://data.london.gov.uk/dataset/smartmeter-energy-consumption-data-in-london-households-vqm0d) · [Trial user guide, §2.1](https://doc.ukdataservice.ac.uk/doc/7857/mrdoc/pdf/7857_userguide.pdf) | Compare the two fixed groups' mean consumption on common dates during high-price hours and afterward. ### Fulton Fish Market — a short demand series with an instrument

Angrist, Graddy and Imbens study daily whiting sales by one dealer. Offshore storms shift supply, giving a candidate instrument for log price in a log-demand equation. Price and quantity are jointly determined. Ordinary PLR adjustment for day of week still requires an assumption about unobserved demand shocks. Instrumental-variable identification instead requires storm conditions to shift price and to affect demand only through the modeled price channel, conditional on controls. The authors discuss heterogeneity in buyers’ price responses. [Original study](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-937X.00141)

Two public files differ materially. The MIT archive’s `fish.dta` has 97 rows, 14 variables and weekday indicators without a calendar-date column. The dated teaching release has 111 trading days from December 2, 1991 to May 8, 1992, with 32 stormy, 34 mixed and 45 fair days. On that release, residualizing log quantity, log price and the storm indicator on a constant and Monday–Thursday indicators gave an OLS elasticity of **−0.563**. The storm-only IV ratio was **−1.119** (reduced form −0.388 divided by first stage +0.346). These are same-day benchmark coefficients. Splitting those 111 dates into five successive roughly equal blocks produced **9, 10, 8, 5 and 0 stormy days**. A final-block storm-specific contrast has no within-block variation. This gives Chapters 4–5 a concrete weak-support exercise before introducing machine-learned nuisances or longer response horizons. [MIT author archive](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/inline-files/fish.dta) · [Dated teaching data, pinned release](https://github.com/ccolonescu/PoEdata/blob/c41bee8fb53ccdec53961fc9fd85b7b84bd17d1c/data/fultonfish.rda)

### Kenya file and sample check

I downloaded the corrected 354.5 MB ZIP and matched its published MD5, `2bc58b30c5f432b12b02b28e07daae43`. Its prepared `code/data/GE_MarketData_Panel_ECMA.dta` contains **3,294 unique market-months**, spanning 61 markets and 54 months. Only **1,734** have the main price index `pidx_wKLPS_med`. The common window from December 2014 to January 2017 gives **1,586 observations** (61 markets × 26 consecutive months), numerically matching the study’s reported count. Four earlier months contribute 32, 32, 42 and 42 observed indices. The authors’ Table 4 driver imposes a January 2017 endpoint on the indices. A researcher who simply drops missing outcomes will still include all 148 early records. [Corrected archive and checksum](https://zenodo.org/records/16548593) · [Published sampling description](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.3982/ECTA17945)

For a forward-fold exercise, record the first month each market was surveyed as well as the month attached to its price. In the prepared file, **183** nonmissing main-index entries precede the market’s first recorded survey month (`first_month_surveyed`). Their historical date alone does not establish that the index was available to a learner in that month. The archive also supplies monthly transfers by two-kilometre distance bands, the authors’ Table 4 price-index script and the instrumental-variable script for Appendix Table H.4. These inputs let a reader compare common-window and staggered-entry scoring and retain the study’s spatial exposure definition. No effect was refitted for this file check. [Authors’ data and analysis code](https://zenodo.org/records/16548593)

## What the original 105-entry ledger measured

Parsing the original May entries gave 105 distinct keys and 97 distinct literal source URLs. Six URLs occur more than once. For example, the FRED-MD and FRED-QD entries point to one provider page, while the two PSID entries describe the same survey. Of the 48 entries tagged `time_series`, 36 carry a verified status. The corresponding counts are 23 of 36 for `tabular` and 20 of 21 for `regression`, totaling 79 verified and 26 unverified. Historical authentication flags are true for 23 entries, false for 81 and absent for one. These are counts of ledger assertions. ## Decision rule for future examples

Before adding another application, name the unit assigned treatment, when treatment is known, the outcome horizon, information available to the nuisance model, and what independent benchmark exists. For a single series record treatment support by fold, overlap between label windows, score dependence and the number of retained dates. For a panel also state whether units, dates, or both must stay together. A known injected effect supports bias and coverage claims; randomized contrasts support treatment-effect comparisons; an observational published estimate supplies a specification benchmark. These distinctions determine what a chapter can claim from a completed run.
