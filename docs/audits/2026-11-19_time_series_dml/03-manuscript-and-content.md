# Manuscript, web edition and teaching materials

The ten chapters give readers a usable progression from partial regression to temporal scores, panel models and dynamic treatment effects. The largest publication risk is the way readers encounter quantitative evidence. A real store panel, an insurance simulation, a published observational estimate and an expected simulation result each answer different questions. Several passages treat agreement with one of these as validation of causal accuracy. Both editions now use measured results for the Chapter 5 coverage example. The November 20 revision also addresses Chapter 4 retail inference and the panel and insurance examples. The cover and broader preface claims remain editorial items. **Chapter 3 at the audit snapshot.** The package bias test treated resampled means as new independent simulation evidence. At nominal 5%, it rejected a known zero mean in 192 of 200 audit experiments. Experiment 3.6's displayed code gave 99.8% detection at 100 draws, compared with 17% in print. The November 20 correction revises both editions, fixes the package test and rescores the recoverable historical verdicts. [Original experiment rerun](04-reproducibility.md) · [Correction and results](08-bias-test-correction.md)

Experiment 3.1's original expected table also changed the interpretation of confounding. It gave the naive OLS bias as +0.847. The runnable specification gave +0.133 with a Monte Carlo SE of 0.125 and RMSE of 1.254 across 100 draws. The generator redraws independent centered treatment and outcome coefficients, so signed errors can cancel across draws. To teach persistent signed bias for one economy, hold the confounding coefficients fixed across simulations and vary the sampled observations. To teach the existing experiment, report the dispersion and direction of errors across economies. The original table also gave 95% coverage for controlled OLS. Its actual interval covered in 69 of 100 runs, rising to 91 on the same draws after including the fitted intercept in the covariance calculation and using the corresponding t critical value. For the forest and boosting rows, match the bootstrap learner to the point estimator before attributing undercoverage to lack of cross-fitting.
