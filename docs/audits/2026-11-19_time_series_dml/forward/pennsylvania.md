# Could an early reemployment-bonus estimate predict later claimants’ earnings?

At a **July 1, 1989** freeze, allowing one additional quarter for wage reporting gave an earlier estimated bonus effect of **−$12** on next-quarter covered earnings. The later randomized comparison gave **+$96**. The frozen prediction minus the later estimate was **−$108** with a 95% interval of **−$517 to +$300**. A constant zero forecast missed by $96. The data permit substantial errors in either direction. [Pennsylvania final report, chapters III and VIII](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-L37-PURL-LPS113875/pdf/GOVPUB-L37-PURL-LPS113875.pdf) · [Claimant records and original codebooks](https://www.upjohn.org/sites/default/files/2019-02/PA_ReempBonus.zip)

**What was randomized.** The main comparison covers everyone selected for the raw high-bonus, long-qualification-window offer with voluntary workshop access (arm 4) against control. The treatment was an opportunity to receive six times the weekly benefit amount upon qualifying for reemployment. Assignment began in July 1988 at Pittston. Eleven further offices entered in October 1988. Allocation proportions changed in July and October 1989. We preserve all 5,472 assignments to these two arms when defining eligible cohorts. Late claimants enter from July 1 through the October 18, 1989 end of assignment, contributing 581 offers and 1,194 controls. [Study design, pp. 36–39 and 88–89](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-L37-PURL-LPS113875/pdf/GOVPUB-L37-PURL-LPS113875.pdf)

**What the outcome measures.** The administrative record `wrwage1` gives nominal earnings paid in the first full calendar quarter following the benefit application by Pennsylvania employers covered by unemployment insurance. It includes zero recorded earnings and omits self-employment and out-of-state work. A flagged zero from one employer can coexist with positive wages reported by another. For the secondary outcome, assignment reduced the proportion remaining in the initial UI spell beyond week six by **4.55 percentage points** among 2,849 earlier claimants. The later estimate was **+0.19 points**. The difference was **−4.74 points [−11.12, +1.64]**. This uses the archive's `inuidur1` and allows nine weeks from application to accommodate the three-week break defining the end of a spell. [Administrative outcome definitions, chapters VII–VIII and appendix B](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-L37-PURL-LPS113875/pdf/GOVPUB-L37-PURL-LPS113875.pdf)

**Whose clock.** Operators assigned cohorts weekly and could observe the eligibility and bonus procedures. Their historical dates of receipt of individual employers’ wage returns are missing. The three availability assumptions above preserve actual application and assignment dates. The two-quarter assumption leaves only Pittston in training, so its statewide comparison also changes the office population. The public archive and the government's published final estimate postdate July 1989. A public analyst's numeric forecast from these records at the time is unverified. The original report describes preliminary findings available internally that influenced the October allocations. [Release record](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/research/publications/pennsylvania-reemployment-bonus-demonstration-final-report) · [Public data archive](https://www.upjohn.org/data-tools/employment-research-data-center/pennsylvania-reemployment-bonus-demonstration)

**Estimation and uncertainty.** Within each cohort we compare assignment arms separately by office and allocation period, weighting cell differences by the combined claimant count. The early estimate is frozen without seeing the later office mix. The main interval combines the two independent cohort variances conditional on the observed strata, using the two arms' within-cell sample variances. Allowing the score contributions to covary within the same calendar selection batch gives an interval of **−$487 to +$270** for the main prediction error. Only 16 later selection dates enter that sensitivity. [Implementation](../external/forward/pennsylvania.py)

**Published comparison.** The original evaluation pooled arms 4 and 6 because workshop attendance was low. Arm 6 ended in July. Rebuilding its Table VIII.3 and Appendix E.6 on the original 13,913 complete eligible cases gives **+$74.14** (SE **$65.14**) for the pooled arm and $1,697.57 for mean control earnings. All five first-quarter effects and standard errors agree to the published dollar. That sample requires a subsequent claim filing. The primary forward comparison uses the continuously offered arm and retains all assigned claimants, including those who never reached the offer stage. The Pennsylvania report is the file `cd/reports/Final_report.pdf` inside the ZIP, also distributed by GovInfo. During the audit the separate “Report” link on Upjohn's landing page opened the Washington experiment.
