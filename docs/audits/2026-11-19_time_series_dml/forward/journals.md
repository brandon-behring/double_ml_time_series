# What did 2018 economics papers actually test against later evidence?

Across three journals’ 2018 print volumes, **158 of 215 items** reported an original empirical finding under the screening rule below. Three of those 158 made a headline projection about a treatment or policy effect. One compared a prediction made before treatment with a separately estimated program response. Five other studies predicted subsequent outcomes. This screen cannot estimate how many economics papers reached incorrect causal conclusions. A later test is informative for a different question in each of these settings. [Paper-level findings, classifications, access evidence and issue links](journals-2018.csv)

## Three comparisons behind the numerator

| Study | What the researchers projected | What was measured afterward | What the comparison establishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fieler, Eslava and Xu, Colombian trade reform | The researchers estimated a trade and quality-upgrading model using plant data before the 1991 reform. | Changes in skill intensity and other outcomes after the reform matched important model predictions. | A genuine pre/post model check. Observed post-reform changes include other contemporaneous influences. A separately identified later treatment contrast would test the reform effect more directly. [Study](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20150796) |
| Chetty and Hendren, neighborhood exposure | County-specific forecasts combine estimates from childhood movers with outcomes of permanent residents. | The authors use historical data to validate the design. Section VI of their paper derives forecast error and precision weights from estimated moments. | A new later cohort with its own benchmark effect was unverified in this screen. The forecasting calculation serves a researcher choosing among noisy county estimates. [Authors’ paper, section VI](https://rajchetty.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/movers_paper2.pdf) |
| Fowlie, Greenstone and Wolfram, weatherization | Pre-retrofit engineering audits projected energy savings for the interventions. | Metered consumption and the authors’ program comparisons produced savings less than one-third of projections in the published paper. | An operational forecast encountered a measurable discrepancy. Random encouragement identifies an offer effect. Comparisons per participating household depend on take-up, treatment definition and additional assumptions. The projection and outcome refer to the studied intervention. [Published paper](https://epic.uchicago.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/5/2019/07/Publication-1-1.pdf) |

Thus **1 of 3** effect-projection papers supplied a comparison to an independently estimated response to the studied intervention. For a stricter test, freeze an earlier *empirical* effect, predict an independently assigned later intervention, and estimate that later effect from its assignment. We did not verify that complete sequence in these three papers. Treating all 158 empirical papers as having promised that test would misstate the denominator. ## Why ordinary chronological validation gives a different count

The main claims divide into **84 historical estimates, 41 follow-ups of an original exposure, 23 model counterfactuals, 5 outcome predictions, 3 effect projections and 2 broad claims about external applicability**. For example, the French canvassing study follows the originally assigned precincts into later elections. The later votes measure persistence after that campaign. A forecast of what another campaign would achieve requires fresh treatment variation. Three-country banking trials provide independent sites, while a defensible historical prediction also requires knowing which results were available before the next assignment. [Canvassing paper](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20160524) · [Banking trials](https://doi.org/10.1257/app.20160597)

The five outcome-prediction studies vary in what was held out. One backtested a variance-trading rule using past market data. A bail study reserved the final six months as one component of a mixed holdout that also contained unused judges and random cases. Its alternative decision policies were simulated. Students’ stated job preferences were compared with jobs reported four years after graduation. Two other papers studied future GDP growth using financial spreads and later electricity-market bids using earlier bidding patterns. A strictly time-exclusive fit of every parameter was unverified for those two. Neither a target date nor an out-of-sample label alone establishes the training cutoff. [Trading](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjx034) · [Bail, Figure I and Table A.8](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5947971/) · [Job choices](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjx035) · [GDP](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20776) · [Market bids](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20160177)

## Public data and the next feasible tests

Every eligible row records an acquisition status separately from the scientific classification. For **12** papers we sampled substantive analysis inputs. For **17** more we retrieved archive bytes without establishing the required observations. **117** have a publisher-linked deposit whose contents or fitness for this specific test remain unverified. Four author deposits returned a guestbook requirement. Two studies publish aggregates while the original linked microdata require another route. One releases retailer aggregates while household histories are restricted. One documents a data application, one releases code with the microdata route unresolved, and three have no verified acquisition path. These are acquisition findings as of this audit. A public deposit made after the study cannot by itself establish what a forecaster could see at a historic cutoff. ## Screening and reproduction

We used the full issue lists for AER, AEJ Applied and QJE and matched DOI, print volume and issue against publisher-deposited Crossref records. AER published a combined issue 4–5. Twenty-two QJE articles in this print roster first appeared online in 2017. Filtering by online year would change the sample. Each CSV row links its issue and the evidence for its principal claim. [AER issues](https://www.aeaweb.org/journals/aer/issues) · [AEJ Applied issues](https://www.aeaweb.org/journals/app/issues) · [QJE volume 133](https://academic.oup.com/qje/issue/133/1)

We removed 15 front-matter entries to yield 200 substantive items for screening. Eligible papers report a new empirical measurement, estimated model or substantive empirical contrast, including new estimates obtained from previously published data. The 158 comprise 75 quasi-experimental studies, 30 estimated structural models, 28 randomized studies, 14 observational studies, six measurement studies, three predictive studies and two combining randomized and observational comparisons. We excluded nine comments or replies, three editorials or lectures, 20 theory papers, seven primarily calibrated model studies and three primarily methodological papers. These judgment calls affect the empirical denominator. For example, the choice-timing study estimates its alternative models on subjects’ observed decisions, while the narrative-sign-restrictions paper principally proposes an identification method. [Choice timing study](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20150742) · [Narrative sign restrictions](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20161852)

The screen records each paper’s principal published claim. We read journal abstracts and author or publisher texts for targeted checks of eligibility, projections, holdouts and data access. The CSV preserves unverified comparisons and data routes explicitly. A negative abstract screen cannot rule out a supporting appendix check. Selected cases received a second read. A field-wide prevalence claim would need a broader sampling frame, a preregistered rubric and independent double-coding of all items. [Full ledger](journals-2018.csv)
