# Could first-season fertilizer use have informed the second offer?

**No.** The western Kenya experiment assigned two rounds of a free-delivery offer for the 2004 long and short rains. The researchers collected the post-treatment household adoption responses in **2005**, after both offers. Even if the first adoption interview had taken place on January 1, 2005 and the second offer as late as December 31, 2004, the response would have been unavailable at assignment. The executable checks these bounds against the text of the original paper. [Study, sections IV and VI](https://www.atai-research.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/aer-Nudging-Fertilizer-2011.pdf) · [Availability check](../external/forward/fertilizer.py)

The authors report that the basic offer increased fertilizer use by **11 percentage points** in the first season and **10.5 points** in the second in their descriptive comparisons. Both contrasts describe an intervention that was randomized. Comparing the two after the 2005 survey addresses repeatability across seasons. Training a model on first-season *use* to forecast the second response would give it information collected later. The 2004 background questionnaire and records of purchases through the program are different measurements. Household adoption includes fertilizer obtained elsewhere. Reassignment also shared households and accounted for prior assignment. [Published study and appendix](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.101.6.2350)
