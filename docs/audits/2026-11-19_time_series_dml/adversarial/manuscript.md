# Which claims can the book and software support?

The editorial priority is to change the recommendation a reader takes away. The book can demonstrate when flexible adjustment pays for itself and teach when an experiment, donor comparison, instrument or simple regression carries the causal argument. The rows below review the ten chapters in the print and web editions and identify the corresponding software or empirical check. The proposed wording and software follow-ups are recommendations from the review.

| Chapter and claim | Evidence, strongest counterargument and recommended treatment |
|---|---|
| **1. Residualization and potential outcomes.** | Keep FWL as the linear baseline. It establishes equality of regression coefficients. Two observationally equivalent mechanisms in the tests produce the same interval around 0.97 and intervention effects of one and zero. State the treatment-assignment assumption whenever giving the slope a causal interpretation. [Scientific tests](../10-property-tests-and-falsification.md) |

State the nuisance-rate and fold/dependence assumptions behind inference. A randomized AIPW score can use known assignment and a simple outcome model. Cross-fitted trees can materially improve the point estimate yet cover the generating slope only 80.6% at nominal 95%. The corrected bias test and 401(k) reproduction are important achievements. DML and six baselines still have different criteria for a PASS; the historical ML bootstrap refits differently configured trees. Use the same truth, rows, squared loss, coverage denominator and estimator in every bootstrap refit before claiming a ranking. Reference agreement on one cross-section is a software check. The scalar sensitivity routine assumes a bias curve from an estimate, SE and arm sizes. Formal matched-study Rosenbaum bounds need the matched design and assignment model. A large reported sensitivity threshold cannot establish price exogeneity. | **5. Temporal PLR and standard errors.** | Keep the comparison of identical fitted scores with and without HAC. In 500 persistent-score simulations HAC raised coverage from 62.0% to 86.4%; the remaining deficit matters. The moderate design yields 187/200 covered intervals with full controls, versus 168/200 with lag-only controls; ten product clusters limit general inference. Product-zero pointwise rolling intervals were too variable to establish the regime shift. No fitted customer or competitor policy effect in these data transfers directly to an insurer. No application here establishes that a learned individualized policy outperforms a uniform policy out of time. The eight runnable companion examples exercise the algorithms and the two notebooks consume or illustrate validation results. The manuscript, web port, notebooks, examples and research ledger can all repeat the same numerical evidence. A successful build or matching coefficient is appropriate evidence for software behavior. Known-truth repeated draws address bias and coverage; fresh randomized assignments support evaluation of later causal effects. ## When to choose another approach

- **A randomized intervention with a known probability.** Estimate the randomized difference, respect the unit of assignment and use pre-treatment prognostic variables for precision. Use weighted/centered scores for repeated randomized decisions. Assess the extra gain from richer models against a fully interacted regression on the same participants. [Lin](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~winston/agnostic.pdf) · [Wager et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5111650/)
- **Observational, repeated exposure with credible conditional exchangeability and support.** Start with a scientifically specified regression, splines and temporal or group-aware folds suited to the question. DML is a serious candidate when flexible nuisance fits can remove meaningful error. Distinguish the variance-weighted PLR slope from the population average effect. Check precision and coverage under processes resembling the application. [Estimator assumptions](../../../../ASSUMPTIONS.md) · [Repeated-draw comparisons](simulation.md)
- **A policy adoption, external shock or endogenous price.** Establish what can support the counterfactual: donor units and pre-fit for synthetic DiD, credible comparison cohorts for staggered DiD, or a relevant and defensible instrument. For an impulse response, compare local projections on common dates and identify the shock. Flexible models can be used inside those designs. They do not supply the missing donor, instrument or exogeneity assumption. [California](../pilots/california.md) · [Fish IV](../06-public-datasets.md#fulton-fish-market--a-short-demand-series-with-an-instrument) · [Italian bank shock](empirical.md)
- **Forecasting an outcome or an effect.** Evaluate outcome predictors against later observations using release-aware rolling origins. To test a forecast of an effect, issue the numerical forecast before the later assignments and compare all methods with the same design-based contrast and its uncertainty. Forward validation can reject a poor transfer assumption even when both past and future experiments are valid. It cannot recover an unobserved policy counterfactual by itself. [GDP example](../forward/gdp.md) · [Frozen effect comparison](../prospective/scoring.md)
