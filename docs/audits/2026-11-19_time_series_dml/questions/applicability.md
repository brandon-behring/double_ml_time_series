# Which other time-series causal questions fit this framework?

Start with the source of variation. A nuisance model can adjust for a rich history or improve precision. The source of variation determines which counterfactual is observable. These three studies illustrate when an existing score can answer the substantive question and when it needs a different research design.

| Question | Observable comparison and time horizon | How to use the framework | Diagnostic that could overturn the interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **How much less fish do buyers purchase when price increases?** Fulton Fish Market | Compare price and quantity on days with different supply conditions. The dated teaching release covers 111 trading days. Price is determined jointly with demand. | First reproduce the ordinary and instrumental-variable estimates on the same days. Flexible prediction can adjust the three series for calendar effects and available histories. Estimating price sensitivity still requires a relevant instrument and a credible exclusion restriction. | Count storm and fair-weather days in each time block, examine the first stage and weak-instrument uncertainty, and check whether storms also change demand or the composition of customers. [Original demand study](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-937X.00141) · [Repository data comparison](../06-public-datasets.md#fulton-fish-market--a-short-demand-series-with-an-instrument) |
| **How much does transit service reduce road congestion?** Los Angeles transit strike | On October 14, 2003, the county system shut down for 35 days. The author's regression discontinuity in calendar time estimates a 47% rise in highway delay at strike onset. The road sensor panel observes one shutdown. | Retain the local time discontinuity and the author's 28-day bandwidth as a reference. Use time-blocked prediction for predetermined route characteristics and calendar patterns. Examine comparable weekdays on either side of onset, and treat the reopening as a separate contrast with its own timing assumptions. | Check other dates for comparable jumps, weather and incident histories near the cutoff, anticipation, sensitivity to bandwidth and the contrast between routes with high and low transit substitution. Road-segment splits that put the same city-day in training and testing would hide common shocks. [Paper and design](https://anderson.are.berkeley.edu/pdf/Anderson%202014.pdf) · [Replication deposit](https://doi.org/10.3886/E112854V1) |
| **Does expanding insurance increase emergency-department use?** Oregon Medicaid lottery | The ballot selected people from a waiting list and allowed members of selected households to apply. Follow-up includes roughly 18 months of emergency-department visits in the Portland-area sample. The authors report +0.41 visits per person for the effect of Medicaid coverage. | For the effect of the **offer**, compare randomized selection conditional on listed household size, use pre-lottery utilization for adjustment and keep households together in folds and inference. For the effect of **coverage**, use selection as an instrument and report the offer effect on enrollment alongside the reduced form. | Verify overlap by household size, linkage and follow-up completeness, and the first stage. The coverage interpretation requires exclusion and monotonicity and pertains to people whose coverage changed with selection. Measuring persistence across periods requires comparable dated outcomes in the chosen data release. [Published design and results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3955206/) · [Author deposit](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/SJG1ED) |

## How far the present evidence goes

**Fish demand has a measured support problem.** On the dated release, weekday-adjusted log-price OLS gives −0.563. The storm-only IV ratio gives −1.119. Storm counts across five successive blocks are 9, 10, 8, 5 and 0. A storm-specific effect in the final block has no observed within-block comparison. These scalars need not agree when prices are endogenous. The 97-row author archive and the dated 111-row teaching file have different samples. [Existing calculations and data provenance](../06-public-datasets.md#fulton-fish-market--a-short-demand-series-with-an-instrument)

**A single policy date needs a credible counterfactual trajectory.** For the transit example, the paper and archive supply a published reference and a design to evaluate. More sensors improve spatial detail. They do not supply additional strike dates. The relevant uncertainty must allow daily shocks and serial dependence. The Los Angeles estimate above is the author's published finding. The five reanalyses in this collection did not refit the strike. [Author paper](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.104.9.2763)

**An offer is directly compatible with the randomized-trial analyses.** Oregon's offer and Medicaid enrollment are distinct treatments. For an offer reanalysis, the household-level folds and assignment-aware adjustment in the water, turnout and electricity examples provide working templates. The same scalar PLR on observed coverage would compare people who made different enrollment decisions. The project's public-use data and analysis programs are catalogued in the NBER and Harvard deposits. We reviewed the design and published targets for this guide. [Oregon archive](https://www.nber.org/research/data/oregon-health-insurance-experiment-data) · [Household trial benchmarks](../11-external-replication-benchmarks.md#randomized-microeconomics-benchmarks)

For a new case, record the target population and units, when treatment was assigned or learned, the outcome window, what was known before assignment, and the effective number of independent assignments or shocks. Compare candidate estimators on identical scored rows first. Report changes in support, sample or target alongside any change in estimates. A precise predictive fit cannot resolve an unmeasured supply shock, a coincident policy change or selective take-up.

## Choose an estimator from the question

| Target | Working route in this repository |
|---|---|
| Effect of today's randomized offer on a prespecified outcome window | Use the known assignment probabilities for a direct estimate. Cross-fit outcome adjustment across independent households or participants. Preserve the timing of pre-decision covariates. [Reminder analysis](reminders.md) |
| Scalar response to a repeated exposure given observed history | A partial-linear score and forward calendar folds can estimate a common slope or a variance-weighted projection. Report treatment support and dependence in the scores. A causal interpretation additionally requires credible conditional assignment and temporal ordering. [Temporal estimator](../../../../dml_ts/dml/temporal_plr_dml.py) |
| Effect of treatment in several successive periods on a terminal outcome | The recursive g-estimator fits period-specific effects under a linear structural nested mean model. Use whole trajectories for panel cross-fitting, define how subsequent treatment follows the intervention, and establish sequential exchangeability and support for the histories being compared. A single price cut or policy rollout does not by itself supply those histories. [Dynamic estimator and model](../../../../dml_ts/dml/dynamic_g_estimation.py) |
| Effect of a policy assigned by place, border or date | Start from the local comparison, discontinuity or randomization that supplies the counterfactual. Keep policy units and repeated observations together where required. Orthogonal adjustment can assess sensitivity to historical controls, but changing the comparison geography can change the answer even with a fixed learner. [Minimum-wage analysis](minimum-wage.md) |
