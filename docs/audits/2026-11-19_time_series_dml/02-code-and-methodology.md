# Estimators, identification and software behavior

The strongest teaching path runs from linear residualization to cross-sectional PLR, then to a clearly specified scalar model for a single ordered series. The highest-priority corrections concern panel preprocessing, the insurance workflow's effect intervals, and the published 401(k) benchmark. Each can produce plausible output while answering a different question from the prose. Findings about core estimators below apply to both audited commits. The branch-specific changes are in validation.

The table below describes the November 19 snapshot. On November 20 the panel estimator and insurance pipeline changed. [Updated designs, empirical counterexamples and reproduction](09-panel-retail-insurance.md)

## Three failures identified in the November 19 snapshot

The November 20 follow-up fixed the bias test. The numerical check now compares the archived formula and the live validator on identical draws. [Corrected results and archived decisions](08-bias-test-correction.md)

Run the following from the repository root using the audit environment. The probes exercise fitted package entry points, the fixed-effects transformation and the actual bias-testing helper. [Executable checks](method_checks.py)

```sh
PYTHONPATH=. OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 DML_N_JOBS=1 python docs/audits/2026-11-19_time_series_dml/method_checks.py
```

**Panel fixed effects and order at the audit snapshot.** For five observations with unit IDs `0,0,1,1,2`, date IDs `0,1,0,1,0` and values `1,2,5,8,16`, the two-way transformation leaves unit means of −1/6, −1/6 and +2/3. One sequential sweep of the two projections suffices in a complete rectangular panel but can leave substantial unit effects in an unbalanced one. The implementation also estimates means from the entire panel before forming folds. Future outcomes can enter the transformed training responses through those means, compromising a past-information interpretation. Full-sample transformations can have a distinct role in retrospective fixed-effects estimators and require their own justification.

On a second, balanced panel of 15 units × 24 periods, the same observations in unit-major order gave **2.0552 (SE 0.0884)** and dropped two entire clusters from inference. Date-major order gave **2.0654 (SE 0.0699)**. Row-position folds score different observations. Even sorting by date can divide a date across training and test when the cut falls inside the date. Revisions should demonstrate fold membership by unique date or unit, according to the intended design, and check that both sets of means vanish on unbalanced inputs. [Fixed-effects and cluster code](../../../dml_ts/dml/temporal_plr_dml.py)

**Insurance demo estimates and units at the audit snapshot.** With a true effect of two, four past-only folds and 400 simulated observations, adding 100 to every binary-treatment outcome moved the estimated effect from **23.23 to 1,063.63**. Prefix rows with no out-of-fold prediction retain an initialized zero for the nuisance predictions; clipping the propensity makes treated observations in that prefix receive a weight of 100. The binary score also uses one marginal outcome nuisance where the usual doubly robust ATE score uses treatment-specific outcome means. Temporal validity and double robustness require separate checks.

For continuous treatment, changing the exposure unit by multiplying all treatments by ten moved the coefficient from **2.0650 to 0.2065** while the reported SE stayed **0.05523**. The pipeline uses the final regression residual as the uncertainty score, omitting the residual-treatment factor and slope normalization. A coefficient SE should scale with the coefficient. The global feature scaler is also fitted ahead of splitting. Use the verified scalar estimator for the numeric teaching example and recheck the pipeline with outcome-shift, unit-rescaling and prefix-coverage tests before presenting its intervals. [Demo pipeline](../../../dml_ts/production/dml_pipeline.py)

## Other findings and validation evidence

**Chapter 3's test of mean simulation bias is severely miscalibrated.** The validation helper generates 1,000 bootstrap means from the original Monte Carlo estimates. Their standard deviation estimates the uncertainty of the original sample mean. The code divides that standard deviation by the square root of 1,000 again and compares the statistic with a t distribution on 999 degrees of freedom. Taking more bootstrap draws can thus make the reported p-value arbitrarily small while the original evidence is fixed.

In **200 independent null experiments**, each with 100 independent standard-normal estimates and true mean zero, the repository's raw bias p-value was below .05 in **192/200 (96%)**. A one-sample t-test on the 100 independent estimates rejected in **6/200 (3%)** on the same experiments. The check holds coverage fixed at 95% to isolate the bias p-value. These are bias-test rejections; the final PASS/WARNING/FAIL label additionally applies practical thresholds and a multiple-testing rule. Repair the test at the level of the original independent simulations and re-evaluate historical p-values. The percentile limits and binomial coverage null are also hardcoded at 95% even though callers can configure `alpha`. [Bias-validation implementation](../../../dml_ts/validation/bias_validation.py) · [Chapter 3 description](../../../chapters/chapter_03.tex) · [Null experiment](method_checks.py)

At configured 90% confidence, 180 successes out of 200 represents the nominal target. The hardcoded 95% null yields an exact binomial p-value of 0.00307; the configured 90% null yields 1. The additional division by the bootstrap count also appears in the OLS, IPW/AIPW and ML baseline verdicts. Review the shared statistical rule alongside the main validator. [OLS baselines](../../../dml_ts/validation/ols_baseline.py) · [IPW and AIPW](../../../dml_ts/validation/ipw_baseline.py) · [ML baselines](../../../dml_ts/validation/ml_baseline.py)

**The controlled OLS comparator omits its fitted intercept from the covariance calculation.** Experiment 3.1 specifies 100 draws of 1,000 observations, five covariates, confounding strength one, a treatment effect of two and seed 42. We reran the package's two OLS validators and replayed the same draws to calculate the treatment-slope SE using the full design matrix. The point estimates agreed. [OLS implementation](../../../dml_ts/validation/ols_baseline.py) · [Comparison runner](chapter3_baselines.py)

| Method | Mean signed error (Monte Carlo SE) | RMSE | Package intervals covering truth |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| OLS with treatment alone | +0.1327 (0.1253) | 1.2538 | 15/100 |
| OLS with treatment and five controls | +0.0180 (0.0090) | 0.0917 | 69/100 |

For the controlled fit, the package estimates the point coefficient with an intercept but inverts the cross-product of the uncentered treatment and controls alone. Its residual variance also counts one too many degrees of freedom. The median reported SE was 62.2% of the full-design SE. Using the full design and its t critical value raised coverage to **91/100**, with a 95% Wilson interval of 83.8% to 95.2% across independent draws. Both package runs returned FAIL. In the linear data generator, every new dataset draws independent, centered treatment and outcome coefficient vectors. Conditional on a realized treatment assignment and covariates with both arms present, averaging over outcome coefficients and noise gives zero expected signed confounding bias for the naive difference. Large errors can still occur in individual datasets, as the naive RMSE shows. [Generator](../../../dml_ts/validation/dgp_generator.py) · [Manuscript interpretation](03-manuscript-and-content.md)

**The ML bootstrap compares different fitted procedures.** The random forest baseline fits trees of depth 10 with a minimum leaf size of five. Its bootstrap fits 50 trees of depth eight with the default leaf size of one. The baseline labeled XGBoost uses scikit-learn's `GradientBoostingRegressor` with depth five, then uses 50 depth-four trees for each bootstrap fit. In both cases the point estimator accepts a configurable tree count. Refit the identical procedure in each bootstrap sample before interpreting confidence interval coverage or ascribing a difference to cross-fitting. [Baseline models and bootstrap constructors](../../../dml_ts/validation/ml_baseline.py)

- **401(k) replication.** At the audit snapshot the package and tests used the wrong published targets, and the code inferred an aggregate SE by averaging individual interval endpoints. The November 20 correction uses the fivefold Table 2B references and inference for the mean fitted effect. The companion still uses eleven effect modifiers, including the wealth measures `nifa` and `tw`. The paper uses nine adjustment variables in its constant-slope model. Direct participation fits and the published eligibility-instrumented participation analysis estimate different quantities. [Repository analysis](../../../dml_ts/validation/empirical_replication.py) · [Fixture tests](../../../test/validation/test_empirical_replication.py) · [Corrected comparison](07-401k-replication.md)
- **Sensitivity.** The Rosenbaum module adapts a sensitivity scale to a scalar effect, SE and group sizes, with approximations for continuous exposures. This is an illustrative diagnostic. A sharp matched-design sensitivity analysis requires assignment and matched-set information. Specify the outcome and exposure contrast before interpreting a robustness threshold. [Sensitivity implementation](../../../dml_ts/sensitivity/rosenbaum.py)
- **Data access.** The OJ loader supplies repeated store/brand observations and transforms price and volume. The FRED loader asks for observations over a requested range and, on a cache miss with caching enabled, separately fetches the full series for storage. A date filter on present-vintage values cannot reproduce the information available on a historic decision date. Backward filling can additionally use a future observation. Use vintage or release-date records for a real-time design. [OJ](../../../dml_ts/data/oj_loader.py) · [FRED](../../../dml_ts/data/fred_loader.py)
- **Validation.** Synthetic cross-sectional, time-series, structural-break, dynamic-panel and insurance generators support controlled experiments. The comparison tools include OLS, IPW and ML baselines, resampling diagnostics, storage and plotting. Record treatment law, bias, empirical variability, SE, coverage, failed runs and Monte Carlo error together. A passing statistical threshold on a particular grid establishes performance on that grid. The hardening branch improves evidence about failure and exclusion, especially in the insurance recovery helper. [Validation implementations](../../../dml_ts/validation/) · [Branch differences](01-inventory-and-history.md)
- **Engineering evidence.** Core formula tests cover SE normalization, degenerate cases and residual variation; golden snapshots constrain version drift in temporalcv. The registry, causal monitor and retraining helpers illustrate workflow plumbing. Passing tests of serialization, monitor behavior or snapshot parity cannot verify the underlying economic identification strategy.
