# How to validate an effect forecast at a decision date

Start with a decision an operator could have made. In the reminder trial that decision is whether to assign an eligible participant an activity prompt. The effect is the difference in next-hour log steps under prompt and no prompt, averaged over eligible decisions during study days 14–97. Forecasting an individual's steps, measuring the benefit of the original rollout over time, and estimating the effect of another prompt each require their own benchmark. [Reminder design and results](reminders.md) · [Target-trial design](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4832051/)

| Claim | Hold out | Compare with |
|---|---|---|
| Later outcome prediction | Outcomes released after a real forecast deadline | Actual outcome in its specified release vintage. The GDP exercise tests this clock. [GDP](../forward/gdp.md) |
| Persistence after assignment | A later follow-up of the original assignment | Paired change in the original randomized contrast with shared units and consistent outcome measurement. [Sierra Leone](external-controls.md) |
| Effect of a fresh assignment | A later cohort or assignment episode | A design-based effect estimate in that episode, with its own sampling error. [Reminders](reminders.md) |
| Gain from acting on the forecast | Decisions and outcomes under a specified policy and budget | Incremental benefit of the frozen rule over a feasible alternative, in common outcome or cost units. [Imai and Li](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2021.1923511) |

## Before the first forecast

1. Write down eligibility, intervention, comparator, assignment unit, treatment history, outcome construction, horizon, and population weights. For repeated assignments specify what happens to subsequent treatment. Record who could know each baseline covariate as well as when each earlier outcome matured, arrived, and could be revised.
2. Predeclare calendar dates, training maturity, baseline forecasts, primary loss, precision requirements, and treatment-support checks. Fix the inner learner and folds using information available at issuance. Use the same forecast dates, supported population, and outcome units for every method. A midpoint-labeled historical fit describes its window. An issuance date states when a forecast can influence a decision.
3. Read design documents and verify assignment codes, sentinel values, units, and the denominator **before any forecast is frozen**. Test the decoder on unavailable periods, genuine zero outcomes, top-coded values, and known source-field combinations. For a new archive, keep later outcome records inaccessible to the fitting stage. Record the protocol first, freeze forecasts next, then release the evaluation outcomes.
4. State two different thresholds if the application supports them. The accuracy margin is the largest forecast miss that is immaterial. The action threshold is the benefit required to justify intervening. The reports use sensitivity grids where those values have no independent justification.

## At every scheduled date

Issue a zero forecast, a historical randomized arm difference, and a DML estimate of the same target. Log training and later cluster counts, arm totals, missingness, and treatment variation. For off-policy weights, record concentration and the times and assignments that produce the largest weights. When treatment probabilities change, compare the method's native target to a common, supported reference population. Later population composition only enters a forecast if it was available at issuance. Preserve origins with no mature labels or too few clusters for inference. [Retail assignment check](external-controls.md) · [Transport assumptions](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32253789/)

Evaluate all forecasts against the same later randomized estimate. Report signed error in natural units and differences in squared error versus both baselines. If the benchmark is conditionally unbiased, its noise cancels from the paired loss difference in expectation. Sampling uncertainty remains; ratios of arm means can also have finite-sample bias under some designs. Cross-validation of the nuisance predictions alone supplies no evidence that a causal forecast will travel. [Experimental benchmarks with noisy effects](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10914338/)

## Interpreting the result

- **Drift.** Estimate the earlier-minus-later difference and its uncertainty. A material change requires a common effect definition and a difference large enough for the decision. Compare composition, treatment probabilities and index construction before attributing a shift to a change in behavior.
- **Precision.** A confidence interval for an earlier effect, an interval for an error against a later randomized estimate, and a prediction interval for an entirely new period answer different questions. Condition on a published, fixed forecast when judging that issuance. Refit the historical learner when studying repeat-sample algorithm performance. Keep shared people, shocks, and training data together across dates. With few periods report individual dates and avoid a claim about a population of calendar periods.
- **Tolerance.** A 90% error interval fully inside a prespecified symmetric margin supports pointwise equivalence at the two one-sided 5% level. An interval that also admits consequential errors leaves equivalence unresolved. A change in significance across two separate fits does not test their difference. Repeatedly looking for the first alarm raises the probability of at least one false alarm. [Equivalence testing](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3450848/) · [Measured false alarms](calibration.md)
- **Decisions.** For an all-or-none intervention, let two frozen rules choose actions a and b in {0,1}, let c be the break-even effect in outcome units, and let Z be the later design-based estimate. The quantity (a − b)(Z − c) estimates incremental benefit per eligible unit under an unbiased benchmark and the specified cost conversion. Individualized policies require policy evaluation at comparable assignment probabilities and capacity. A trial's earnings contrast alone cannot establish program net benefit.

Preserve the first forecast and its performance when source interpretation changes after unblinding. Record corrected code, estimates, and reasons alongside the first issuance. A local digest makes later changes detectable against an independently retained copy. Its timestamp cannot establish that a historical forecast was publicly issued before the trial.
